What ReWatch is

Three Raspberry Pis and two ESP32s watch the IoT layer of a building. ReWatch detects network attacks and abnormal device behaviour, scores them into one number, alerts the owner by e-mail and the shelf LED/OLED, and — if nobody answers in time — responds on its own. Nothing here talks to a cloud.

The score

score = clamp( max(condition points) + Σ impulse points · ½(age / 60s) , 0, 100 )

Conditions last while a detector says they are true — a device in warn or critical. Impulses are one-off detections that fade over a 60-second half-life, so one nmap run peaks and then decays on its own.

The acknowledgement rule

This laptop demonstration is simulated. Ten devices appear at 20 s after start/Replay, the AC spike starts at 30 s, and RED opens a 20-second acknowledgement window. Production defaults to 120 s. Black Hat feed injects synthetic detections only.

Refresh reconnects without resetting the scene. Replay clears the scene but not SQLite-retained reports or the five-minute summary cadence. Select topology tiles for reported evidence and explicitly permitted synthetic controls.

When the band goes RED an incident opens with a countdown:

  • You press Acknowledge on the dashboard (or the e-mail link) → the automation is cancelled; you own it.
  • Nobody answers before the countdown ends → the band goes PURPLE and ReWatch attempts the simulated playbook. A missed ACK is not proof of an attack.

The shelf indicator (ESP32-C3)

A NeoPixel carries the band in colour and motion; a 128×64 OLED shows the score, the fleet (which Pis are reporting), the status word and an ECG trace whose rate is the band (flat green, one beat yellow, two red, four purple). If the Pi 4 link goes quiet the board shows a sleeping face and the pixel turns blue — never a stale green.

E-mail (Pi 3 Herald)

Delivery is disabled by default: RED, ACK, PURPLE, resolution, test messages and five-minute summaries produce labelled previews. Explicitly configured SMTP/TLS uses a background worker; server acceptance is not proof of inbox delivery. Never record or commit credentials.

Evidence

Every message on the bus is logged to the pen drive on the Sentinel Pi, one folder per day (telemetry.jsonl, events.jsonl, bus.jsonl). The Pi 4 keeps a rolling SQLite copy for the dashboard and the 5-hour report. If the stick is missing the fleet panel shows usb missing and logging falls back to the SD card.
